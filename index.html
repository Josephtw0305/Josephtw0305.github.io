The question I work on

When do rational agents keep to the rules without ground truth or a trusted intermediary?

  • Mechanism Design
  • Multi-Agent Systems
  • Decentralized Systems

I defended my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Waterloo in June 2026, advised by Prof. Wojciech Golab. My thesis studies incentive constraints and equilibrium in decentralized settings.

My research sits where mechanism design, multi-agent systems, and decentralized systems meet. I design incentives for strategic agents and characterize the equilibria they sustain when no single party can verify the truth or enforce the rules, across aggregation, consensus, and payment settings.

I also work with Ripple's University Blockchain Research Initiative (UBRI) as a Research Analyst, connecting university research with industry questions in digital assets, Layer 2 systems, zero-knowledge proofs, and payment infrastructure.

Before my Ph.D., I spent six years building banking systems at E.SUN Commercial Bank and CTBC Bank, including anti-money laundering, foreign exchange, and treasury risk controls. I still read a mechanism the way a bank reads a control, starting from who has a reason to break it.

I review for IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security, IEEE Transactions on Services Computing, and Blockchain: Research and Applications.

Research

  1. i.

    Truthful reporting without verification

    How to reward honest reports when nobody can check the answer. I derive reward and penalty conditions under which informative reporting is incentive compatible and individually rational, with applications to misinformation and digital trust.

    Binary aggregation, 2026 · Fake news prevention, 2022

  2. ii.

    Credit and settlement without custody

    When a non-custodial payment system can extend credit beyond posted collateral. I characterize how bounded exposure, verifiable settlement, and continuation value deter strategic default, and test the design on an Arbitrum prototype.

    Credit limits, 2026

  3. iii.

    Incentives and verification in blockchain infrastructure

    Validator strategies in Ethereum 2.0 as a Bayesian game, zero-knowledge provenance for critical-minerals supply chains, and a systematization of research themes across Ripple's university network.

    Validator strategies, 2024 · OreProof, 2027 · UBRI research themes, 2026

My current work carries these tools to AI agents that increasingly report, coordinate, and transact on their own, asking who verifies them and who answers when something fails.

OreProof: Verifiable Provenance with Limited Disclosure for Critical-Minerals Supply Chains Using Zero-Knowledge Proofs

Oleksandr Hrabar, Hossein Arshadi Soufiani, Henry M. Kim, Chien-Chih Chen, Ali Vazirizadeh, and Hjalmar Turesson

Proceedings of the 60th Hawaii International Conference on System Sciences

pdf arXiv

Critical-minerals supply chains face a structural tension: regulators and buyers demand verifiable provenance, yet upstream actors are hesitant to disclose supplier identities, assay grades/yields, and prices that verification appears to require. We report a design science account of OreProof, a prototypical traceability platform addressing this verifiability-disclosure trade-off. Instantiated for gold, OreProof combines a hybrid on-chain/off-chain data model, Groth16 zero-knowledge proofs for selective disclosure, a Merkle-batched anchoring pipeline, and UNTP-aligned verifiable credentials on a public zkEVM testnet. Against a transparent baseline, directly inferable confidential attributes fell from three of four categories to none under a defined attacker model, while batched anchoring substantially improved throughput. Our contributions are the artifact prototype as well as four nascent design principles: prove over committed data rather than exposing it; credential only verifiable origin and flag unknown inputs for blended commodities; emit standards-aligned credentials from the outset; and partition disclosure by supply-chain role.

A Tunable Incentive Mechanism for Binary Aggregation Without Verification

Chien-Chih Chen and Wojciech Golab

arXiv preprint arXiv:2606.30974

pdf arXiv

Binary aggregation without verifiable ground truth arises when agents' reports must be aggregated without access to gold-standard labels. This paper studies a tunable reward–penalty mechanism for binary aggregation without verification. Agents choose between a conforming strategy, which reports an informative private signal, and a non-conforming strategy, which follows a deterministic prior-informed report rule. For this mechanism, we derive cost-adjusted sufficient conditions for incentive compatibility and individual rationality as bounds on the reward–penalty ratio. The analysis identifies feasible ratio regions, cases in which ratio adjustment restores feasibility, and parameter regimes in which no ratio satisfies both constraints under the modeled construction. We also state a conditional all-conforming Nash equilibrium result within the restricted strategy set. Entropy-based scaling and stake-weighted redistribution are treated as extensions, with stake-weighted redistribution inducing agent-specific incentive constraints. Numerical checks support the closed-form Tier 1 quantities and illustrate threshold sensitivity.

Credit Limits beyond Full Collateralization in Decentralized Micropayments: Incentive Conditions

Chien-Chih Chen and Wojciech Golab

arXiv preprint arXiv:2604.25913

pdf arXiv

In decentralized non-custodial micropayments, the central challenge is not whether payments can be executed directly, but under what conditions such systems can offer credit limits without requiring full collateral backing. Existing approaches typically tie available credit to posted collateral, causing liquidity requirements to scale with transaction volume and settlement exposure and limiting the practical usefulness of credit-based micropayments. This paper characterizes the incentive conditions under which credit-based non-custodial micropayments can operate beyond full collateralization while remaining incentive compatible. We model repeated buyer–merchant interactions under public monitoring and identify the roles of bounded exposure, verifiable settlement outcomes, and continuation value in deterring strategic default under non-custodial execution. The resulting characterization clarifies the trade-off between capital efficiency and the enforcement conditions required to sustain under-collateralized credit expansion without custodial trust. As an illustrative application-layer instantiation, an Arbitrum Nitro prototype provides execution-level evidence that the settlement, commitment, and incentive-enforcement paths of a credit-limit-based design can be realized with low on-chain overhead.
